The nanoseconds-per-command figure above is a browser number, and browsers cannot measure this. performance.now() is deliberately coarsened to defeat timing side channels — typically 100 µs to 1 ms — against operations that take tens of nanoseconds. So this page times a whole batch between two clock reads and divides, which the resolution does support, and does not show per-operation percentiles at all, because it genuinely cannot measure them. WebAssembly also runs several times slower than native here. The native p50/p99/p99.9, measured with a timer that resolves 41.67 ns and reported with the CPU, OS, compiler and flags that produced them, are in docs/benchmarks.md.

What you are looking at. Synthetic order flow — a narrow price band and three accounts, so orders actually collide — driven through the real engine. Changing the self-trade policy or the risk limits changes the behaviour of the same code paths the test suite exercises; trip the kill switch and new orders are refused while cancellation keeps working, which is the asymmetry explained in docs/risk.md.
